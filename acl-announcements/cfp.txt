The language technologies (LTs) that were applied in response to the Haitian
        earthquake of 2010 showed to the world the life-saving potential for these technologies,
        where their application was responsible for saving 1,000s of lives. Most concerning is
        that in a world that is hit by more than 350 disasters in an average year, the most
        severe of these disasters occur in regions where English is not spoken. With the
        development of massively popular AI systems used as general-purpose machines, the
        potential of LTs in disaster preparedness and response has rapidly grown.
        In response, the focus of this workshop is on the direct application of LTs in crisis
        preparedness and response, including recent work on multilingual and multimodal LT.

        We invite submissions to the <strong>First Workshop on Language Technologies for Crisis
        Preparedness and Response (LT4CPR)</strong> at AACL 2026. The workshop focuses on
        advancing speech and language technologies that support crisis preparedness, crisis
        response, communication in crises and disaster risk reduction, and aims to bring
        together LT research communities with crisis management stakeholders (such as
        practitioners, NGOs, and industry) to bring to the fore interdisciplinary solutions that
        have real-world impact.


   In this first edition, we especially welcome work on:

      <ul>
        <li>
          <strong>Identifying Community Needs:</strong> The crisis ecosystem is quite diverse, composed
          by affected populations, policy-makers, NGOs, industry, public officers,
          and emergency management.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Interdisciplinary approaches for analysis, design, and evaluation of LT systems:</strong>
          insights from communication studies, social sciences, psychology, among others,
          on how to design and evaluate trustworthy crisis technology that fits stakeholders needs.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Translating LT research into operational tools:</strong> how to go beyond AI model
          development to user adoption of technology in high-stakes scenarios.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Ethics and societal implications of AI</strong> in crisis communication and management.
        </li>
      </ul>

      <em>Topics of Interest</em>

      We invite submissions that fall under the following list (but not limited to):

      <ul>
        <li>Research work on AI for crisis response, such as information extraction (e.g., NER and geolocation), situational awareness (e.g., automated triage), and more</li>
        <li>Qualitative studies to understand user needs and guide technology design</li>
        <li>Multilingual and multimodal research on crisis and humanitarian initiatives</li>
        <li>Evaluation and deployment considerations</li>
        <li>Automatic approaches to enhance or facilitate communication from and to communities during crisis (e.g., computational social media and news analysis, summarization, translation, etc.)</li>
        <li>Position papers and literature surveys on current trends, challenges and future directions</li>
        <li>Abstracts for talk proposals or field reports from practitioners, NGOs, humanitarian organizations, and field experts, including experience reports</li>
      </ul>

      <em>Submission Types</em>

      The workshop will accept two types of papers:

      <ol>
        <li>
          <strong>Archival submissions:</strong> Papers of up to 8 pages of content, plus unlimited
          pages for limitations, references and appendices. Accepted papers will be given an
          additional page of content to address reviewer comments. Previously published papers
          cannot be accepted.
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Non-Archival submissions:</strong> Up to 8 pages, excluding references, to
          summarise and showcase in-progress work and work published elsewhere. These papers
          may be presented at the workshop but will not appear in the proceedings.
        </li>
      </ol>
