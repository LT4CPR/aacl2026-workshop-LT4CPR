About the speaker

Professor Federico M. Federici is Director of the Centre for Translation Studies at University College London and a Professor of Intercultural Crisis Communication at the university. Dr. Federici has published extensively on language translation in crises, with his current research centered on multilingual communication in crises, in particular, translated communication in medical and healthcare contexts. He was a Principal Investigator on the INTERACT project, a large multi-year EU-funded project, partnering with colleagues at Dublin City University and other European institutions, with a research focus on crisis translation. Dr. Federici's work also extends beyond crises, looking at the use of translation in doctor-patient interactions, in particular, in scenarios where patients have limited English proficiency, and the impact that translation (both human-mediated and automated) has on patient care and outcomes.

Call for Papers

The language technologies (LTs) that were applied in response to the Haitian earthquake of 2010 showed to the world the life-saving potential for these technologies, where their application was responsible for saving 1,000s of lives. Most concerning is that in a world that is hit by more than 350 disasters in an average year, the most severe of these disasters occur in regions where English is not spoken. With the development of massively popular AI systems used as general-purpose machines, the potential of LTs in disaster preparedness and response has rapidly grown. In response, the focus of this workshop is on the direct application of LTs in crisis preparedness and response, including recent work on multilingual and multimodal LT.

We invite submissions to the First Workshop on Language Technologies for Crisis Preparedness and Response (LT4CPR) at AACL 2026. The workshop focuses on advancing speech and language technologies that support crisis preparedness, crisis response, communication in crises and disaster risk reduction, and aims to bring together LT research communities with crisis management stakeholders (such as practitioners, NGOs, and industry) to bring to the fore interdisciplinary solutions that have real-world impact.

In this first edition, we especially welcome work on:

  • Identifying Community Needs: The crisis ecosystem is quite diverse, composed by affected populations, policy-makers, NGOs, industry, public officers, and emergency management.
  • Interdisciplinary approaches for analysis, design, and evaluation of LT systems: insights from communication studies, social sciences, psychology, among others, on how to design and evaluate trustworthy crisis technology that fits stakeholders needs.
  • Translating LT research into operational tools: how to go beyond AI model development to user adoption of technology in high-stakes scenarios.
  • Ethics and societal implications of AI in crisis communication and management.

Topics of Interest

We invite submissions that fall under the following list (but not limited to):

  • Research work on AI for crisis response, such as information extraction (e.g., NER and geolocation), situational awareness (e.g., automated triage), and more
  • Qualitative studies to understand user needs and guide technology design
  • Multilingual and multimodal research on crisis and humanitarian initiatives
  • Evaluation and deployment considerations
  • Automatic approaches to enhance or facilitate communication from and to communities during crisis (e.g., computational social media and news analysis, summarization, translation, etc.)
  • Position papers and literature surveys on current trends, challenges and future directions
  • Abstracts for talk proposals or field reports from practitioners, NGOs, humanitarian organizations, and field experts, including experience reports

Submission Types

The workshop will accept two types of papers:

  1. Archival submissions: Papers of up to 8 pages of content, plus unlimited pages for limitations, references and appendices. Accepted papers will be given an additional page of content to address reviewer comments. Previously published papers cannot be accepted.
  2. Non-Archival submissions: Up to 8 pages, excluding references, to summarise and showcase in-progress work and work published elsewhere. These papers may be presented at the workshop but will not appear in the proceedings.

Presentation Mode

All accepted papers must be presented at LT4CPR 2026 to appear in the proceedings. Both in-person and virtual presentation options are available.

We invite researchers, students, practitioners, humanitarian organizations, and industry teams to participate in the LT4CPR Shared Task on Automatic Situation Report (Sitrep) Generation, organized as part of the First Workshop on Language Technologies for Crisis Preparedness and Response (LT4CPR) at AACL-IJCNLP 2026, on November 9, in Hengqin, Zhuhai, China.

During a crisis, responders must rapidly synthesize large volumes of fragmented, repetitive, uncertain, and sometimes conflicting information. Social media can provide timely reports about hazards, impacts, infrastructure damage, affected populations, urgent needs, and response activities, but turning these messages into an operationally useful overview remains labor-intensive. This shared task explores how language technologies can support the generation of concise, structured, and verifiable situation reports from crisis-related social media streams.

Task Overview

Participating systems will receive a collection of crisis-related social media messages and generate a situation report that summarizes the most important information contained in the collection.

  • Input: a set of crisis-related social media messages.
  • Output: a structured situation report following the official task template and output format.
  • Goal: produce a report that is informative, concise, well organized, faithful to the source messages, and useful for situational awareness.

Reports may cover information such as the crisis context, major events, affected people and locations, casualties, displacement, infrastructure and service disruptions, emerging needs, response activities, resource provision, and unresolved or uncertain claims. The final schema and required sections will be specified in the task guidelines.

No prior participation in LT4CPR is required, and interdisciplinary teams are especially encouraged.

How to Participate

  1. Register your team using the official registration form.
  2. Review the task guidelines, data license, and participation rules.
  3. Download the released data and baseline resources.
  4. Develop and evaluate your system using the training and development sets.
  5. Submit system outputs for the official test set through the designated platform.
  6. Submit a system description paper if required for inclusion in the shared task proceedings and workshop program.

Participation in the shared task does not require a separate submission to the workshop's general Call for Papers. Shared task system papers will follow their own submission instructions and deadlines.

System Description Papers

Participating teams will be invited to submit a system description paper explaining their approach, resources, experiments, results, error analysis, limitations, and ethical considerations. Papers should clearly disclose the models, external data, APIs, retrieval sources, and manual interventions used by the system.

  • Submission format: official ACL style; up to 8 pages of content, plus unlimited pages for limitations, references, and appendices.
  • Accepted papers: accepted papers will be given one additional page of content to address reviewer comments.
  • Prior publication: previously published papers cannot be accepted.
  • Submission deadline: September 15, 2026, 11:59 p.m. AoE.
  • Submission portal: system paper submission instructions and OpenReview link.
  • Presentation: accepted system papers may be presented as posters or sessions at the LT4CPR workshop.
  • Proceedings: publication details will be announced with the system-paper call.

Participation Rules

  • Each team must designate a primary contact and use a consistent team name across registration and submissions.
  • Participants must comply with the dataset license and may not redistribute restricted task data.
  • All external data, pretrained models, APIs, prompts, and human involvement must be documented.
  • Submitted outputs must be produced by the registered system by the submission deadline.
  • Teams must follow the submission limits and resource restrictions of their selected track.
  • The organizers reserve the right to inspect submissions for rule compliance and to exclude invalid entries.

Ethics and Responsible Use

Crisis-related social media can contain sensitive personal information, distressing content, rumors, and references to vulnerable populations. Participants must use the data only for the purposes allowed by the dataset license and should avoid attempts to identify, contact, or profile individuals represented in the data. Teams should discuss privacy, representational harms, hallucination risks, model limitations, and the risks of deploying automated reports in high-stakes environments.

Shared-task outputs are research artifacts and must not be treated as verified operational intelligence or used as a substitute for expert review and established emergency-management procedures.

Camera-Ready Q&A

When are camera-ready submissions due?

The deadline for research and shared-task papers has been extended from October 10 to Monday, October 12, 2026 (23:59 AoE).

How should I format the final version?

Submit the final, non-anonymous version with all author names and affiliations restored and line numbers removed. Use the unmodified official ACL style files in final mode; if your template uses \aclfinalcopy, uncomment it. The paper must use A4 paper in two columns, with all fonts embedded and no page numbers.

What is the camera-ready page limit?

We suggest 8 pages of main text for camera-ready papers. Accepted archival papers may include up to one additional page to address reviewer comments, for a maximum of 9 pages of main text, plus unlimited pages for limitations, references and appendices, consistent with the workshop's published page limits. Non-archival papers may use up to 8 pages, excluding references.

Which files should I submit?

Submit both the final PDF and the corresponding LaTeX or Word source.

What should I check before submitting?

Run a quick check with ACL pubcheck before sending your files, and resolve any formatting issues it identifies. Confirm that author names and affiliations are present, fonts are embedded, and line numbers and page numbers are absent.
